# Cryptogram source texts — one per line, `#` starts a comment.
#
# Rules the build script enforces, so break them freely and it will tell you:
#   * letters, spaces, commas and a final full stop only — no apostrophes, so
#     no contractions and no possessives
#   * no proper nouns; every word must exist in the codeword vocabulary, which
#     is what lets the solver prove a text has exactly one reading. That
#     vocabulary is English plus American, so British-only spellings are out —
#     CENTRE and RECOGNISE were both rejected on the first pass
#   * avoid heavily repetitive lines. "Better late than never, but never late
#     is better than both" was rejected as ambiguous even with seven starters:
#     repeating the same few words gives the solver almost nothing to work with
#   * long enough, and with enough distinct letters, to be solvable at all —
#     a short line with few letters has many valid decodings
#
# These are starter texts. Proverbs are nobody's property and plain facts are
# not copyrightable; Noah and his wife are adding their own lines later.

# ── proverbs and sayings ────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Do not count your chickens before they are hatched.
People who live in glass houses should not throw stones.
A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step.
The grass is always greener on the other side of the fence.
Absence makes the heart grow fonder, or so the saying goes.
A stitch in time saves nine, and a little care saves plenty.
Too many cooks will spoil the broth, however good the soup.
The early bird catches the worm, but the second mouse wins.
You can lead a horse to water, but you cannot make it drink.
Every cloud is supposed to have a silver lining somewhere.
Do not judge a book by its cover, however tempting that is.
A bird in the hand is worth two of them hiding in the bush.
When the cat is away, the mice will play for hours on end.
Never trouble trouble until trouble happens to bother you.
Actions speak louder than words, and last a great deal longer.
Do not throw the baby out with the bath water in your haste.
There is no use crying over milk that has hit the kitchen floor.
Great minds think alike, though fools seldom differ either.
Do not put all of your eggs into one basket, however sturdy.
A watched pot never boils, and a watched clock never moves.

# ── science and nature ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
An octopus has three hearts, blue blood, and no bones at all.
Honey never spoils, even after thousands of years in a jar.
Sharks were swimming in the sea long before trees existed.
Water expands as it freezes, which is the reason ice floats.
A single bolt of lightning is hotter than the surface of the sun.
Bananas count as berries, but strawberries do not count at all.
Sound travels about four times faster in water than in air.
The moon drifts a little further away from us every single year.
Bees are able to tell human faces apart, which seems unlikely.
On the planet nearest the sun, one day lasts longer than a year.
Trees warn each other about hungry insects through their roots.
The deepest trench in the ocean is deeper than any mountain is tall.
A hummingbird has to eat almost constantly to stay in the air.
Your bones are, weight for weight, stronger than solid steel.
Butterflies taste the world using sensors in their own feet.
A cloud can weigh many hundreds of tons and still float above us.
The middle of our planet is about as hot as the surface of the sun.
Sea otters hold hands while sleeping so they do not drift apart.
A day on our planet is slowly getting longer as the moon retreats.
Owls cannot move their eyes, so they turn their whole head instead.
Some trees alive today were seedlings before the pyramids were built.
The oceans hold almost all of the water on the entire planet.
Ice is slippery because a thin film of water forms on top of it.
Glass is not a liquid, whatever your teacher may have told you.
